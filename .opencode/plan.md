Status: P1 (architecture & component selection) **complete** — architecture as of 2026-09-08, component selection as of 2026-10-01. The board class is **closed and purchased (2026-10-03): the wearable is a Raspberry Pi 5, 8 GB, bought at ₱6,000**, and **no alternative board class is carried** — the flip path is deleted and [README §7](../README.md#7-risks-and-limitations) risk 12 replaces it ([README §3](../README.md#3-hardware) constraint 4; [`docs/hardware.md`](../docs/hardware.md) §4). The 8 GB SKU changed **nothing** in the plan's engineering claims: the resident workload was already shown to fit 4 GB, so 8 GB is a strict superset of every budget, threshold, risk, and evidence class. The vision tier's camera count is **1 or 2, still undecided** — decided at P2 from the FOV-geometry and co-residence analysis, confirmed at P4, and ₱0 to reduce while neither module is bought ([README §3](../README.md#3-hardware)). **Every other cart row is still to buy** (≈ ₱15,800–31,300; itemized total ≈ ₱21,800–37,300) pending the purchase-approval sign-off; the board is booked against the bare board, to be re-split if the PSU or case turns out to have been included. At ₱6,000 the board is treated as a **grey import with no manufacturer warranty**, so a dead-on-arrival is a local replacement purchase and a logged deviation. **Verification is also decided: no hardware bench phase** (2026-10-01) — the T0–T8 thresholds are retained as the acceptance specification and each is discharged at P2 by software or datasheet evidence in the fixed order `[SIM]` → `[BUD]` → `[PROP]` → `[CITE]`, with the component datasheet carrying any claim that has no software method; the device self-measures in use at P4/P5 via the retained `bench-log` and per-update active-tier telemetry; P3's exit is a functional smoke check, not a parity re-run. Selections, quantities, and PH-local costs: [`docs/hardware.md`](../docs/hardware.md) (pointer + wearable; board-agnostic rows vs the selected class rows; the closed board class in §4). The complete cart: [`docs/purchase-list.md`](../docs/purchase-list.md) — **spent ₱6,000** (the board), **≈ ₱15,800–31,300 still to buy**, §10 holding the received-goods inspection (lane A for the board, lane B for every later delivery). [`docs/bench.md`](../docs/bench.md) is now the bring-up/incoming-inspection checklist, with its former ≈ ₱1,600–3,500 breadboard/fixture/prop slice **recorded but not bought**. Open lanes: the co-researcher's **purchase-approval sign-off** for the remaining rows, then arrival. Purchasing constraints that bind the order: tracking hardware is mandatory (part of the final design of both devices), modules ordered connector-ready/pre-fitted, and each component category is bought once — **that rule is now partly spent**, so later electronics spend is a re-buy needing an explicit exception and a swapped part is a logged deviation. The plan carries no "frozen / core / locked" framing — everything stays open to change up to purchase; the baseline-commit evaluation protocol is retired (2026-10-01), and the change procedure (dependency chains, honest impact framing, write-only-on-explicit-instruction) is in [`instruction.md`](instruction.md). Marker scheme decided 2026-09-21 (reversing the 2026-09-15 two-LED beacon): a printed ArUco/AprilTag on the pointer shell, overwriting **position only** — the tag's PnP orientation is ignored (orientation stays on the pointer IMU over the device-to-device link); dictionary and tag size are pinned at P2, with a low-light/contrast check evidenced there and observed at P4 ([§3.8](../README.md#38-pointer-tracking-hardware--notes--contingencies)). P2 (verification by software & datasheet) needs no hardware and does no soldering; [`docs/bench-tests.md`](../docs/bench-tests.md) holds the acceptance specification (T0–T8 thresholds) and the evidence program (S0–S8, tagged by class) with the automated logging pipeline added 2026-09-27 (event stream → capture → reduce → the mandated per-session CSV + acceptance check; the `bench-log` module joins README §4.1) — its wiring maps are now a **P3 carrier-board design entry** reviewed at the P2 exit. **Assembly & outsourcing (decided 2026-09-26, [README §3.9](../README.md#39-assembly--outsourcing-p3))**: the P3 soldered build of both devices is designed here and **soldered by an external local hand-solder service** — schematics, KiCad carrier boards (hand-solderable by design, through-hole-dominant, no fine-pitch SMT on our boards), harness/wiring design, and assembly drawings in-house; bare PCBs fab-ordered **with spares**; everything returning from the service passes in-house **incoming inspection before any power-up**, then the **functional smoke check** (not the retired T0–T8 parity re-run); the one-time full soldering kit shrinks to a **minimal rework & inspection set** ([README §3.4](../README.md#34-assembly--bring-up-tools-one-time)); the operational protocol owns [`docs/assembly.md`](../docs/assembly.md); service quotes/booking land inside the P2 window and fold into the co-researcher's purchase-approval sign-off ([README §9](../README.md#9-open-items)); P3 window (Nov 2–21), exit (smoke check), and every defense date are unchanged ([`docs/schedule.md`](../docs/schedule.md) ladder item 7). Open items: purchase-approval sign-off for the remaining cart rows, the assembly-service selection, and the PCB-fab order (see [§9 Open items](../README.md#9-open-items)).
