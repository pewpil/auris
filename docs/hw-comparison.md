# Cane — Hardware branch comparison (esp32-s3 / esp32-p4 / raspi-5 / raspi-4)

> **Derived from the four branch tips (2026-09-26)** — `esp32-s3` (`b853b5b`), `raspi-5` (`adade7f`), `esp32-p4` (`490a7fc`), `raspi-4` (`76e8797`) — each carrying its own itemized components-and-pricing exercise over the `agnostic` hardware-open baseline (`5b40cb2`). Purpose: give the co-researcher's purchase-approval sign-off ([README §9](../README.md#9-open-items)) a single comparison artifact across **all** decision variables (cost, heat, power, compute, cameras, link, form — the full set of 27 below), with a 1/0/−1 score matrix: **1 = the branch's wearable board performs well on the variable, 0 = neither good nor poor, −1 = performs poorly** — absolute scores, so several boards can earn 1s together on a row. The branch choice itself is part of the sign-off; this matrix is a decision aid, not the decision.

**Comparison boundary.** The pointer and both P5-bound one-time blocks are byte-identical across the four tips (the ESP32-S3 pointer kit — devkit, IMU, time-of-flight module, ultra-wideband tag, buttons, earphones — plus bench infrastructure, harness kits, the printed-marker stock, the carrier-PCB and soldering-service quote lanes). Every difference priced or scored below is the **wearable side**: its compute board, cameras, audio path, and worn power chain.

## 3. Explanation of each variable

1. **Whole-project cost envelope** — each branch's committed fixed-price total from its purchase list (notes row N3). Scored by where the envelope sits, not by a relative race: S3's sits comfortably low (+1), the P4's and the Pi 4's are reasonable middles (0 each — the Pi 4 clearly above the P4, both clearly below the S3), the Pi 5's is more than double the S3's (−1).
2. **Bench-phase outlay** — what must be spent up front for the breadboard phase (the first gate of the schedule). The bench proves the same functions on all four, so paying for a 45 W supply and bank-class power before that proof is a poor property (−1), mid-size board classes are neutral (0 — P4 and Pi 4), a ₱500 devkit is well (+1).
3. **Wearable-core part cost** — the only part of the bill that differs by branch (notes N4). Same scoring logic: the microcontroller-class cores score well or neutral; the Pi 4's ~₱6.8–10.5k is half the Pi 5's but 4–5× the S3's → 0.
4. **Supply stability** — how risky the branch-critical rows are to buy today under the plan's local-lane purchasing rule: S3 confirmed in stock locally (+1); P4 listed but its own document flags single-shop risk (0); the Pi 5's official distributor is currently showing sold-out with an explicit supply advisory (−1); the Pi 4 is genuinely well stocked on multiple local lanes, with only boilerplate advisory wording → 0.
5. **Compute headroom at the co-residence gate** — the Phase 2 bench test 7 makes the audio renderer and the camera-vision pointer tracker share one chip. S3: expected to pass, but the margin is exactly what the plan's risk 8 worries about → 0. P4 (400 MHz, up to 32 MB PSRAM), R5 (Linux + OpenCV-class compute), and the Pi 4 (4× A72 + Linux) all carry genuinely comfortable margins (+1 each). The detection-software dimension is separated into row 27; this row stays on raw margin.
6. **Motion-to-sound latency certainty** — the plan's absolute 100 ms motion-to-sound target, of which ≤ 5 ms is the per-audio-buffer render row. S3: literal hardware path, but the margin must be proven on the bench — scored 0 (unguaranteed, unthreatened). P4: literal path and more margin → 1. R5 and the Pi 4: a kernel scheduler sits in the audio path — the raspi-5 branch names Linux audio jitter as its real exposure, and the raspi-4 branch inherits exactly the same Linux class → −1 each.
7. **Render underrun risk** — the same render row from the failure side: buffer underruns make the sound stutter. Scoring mirrors row 6 for all four branches.
8. **Boot and run reliability** — participants in the Phase 5 study put this on their heads: S3 and P4 power on into the sound in under a second with no operating system to freeze (+1 each); both Pi branches take tens of seconds and introduce crash/silent-failure modes → −1 each.
9. **Heat at the head** — sustained render + camera load next to skin. Both microcontroller-class boards run cool without heatsinks (+1 each); both Linux boards want cooling near the user's face — the Pi 4 most of all, since its branch makes the heatsink mandatory outright → −1 each.
10. **Thermal-design burden on the printed build** — how much the enclosed, service-soldered Phase 3 housing must engineer airflow: none needed for S3 (+1); mild vent/area planning for the bigger P4 board (0); deliberate thermal design for both Pi branches — the raspi-4 branch's own text calls it a stronger duty than the raspi-5's A76 → −1 each.
11. **Mobile-rail weight and bulk** — the worn supply's mass: small protected cell (+1); pocket banks on P4 and Pi 4 (0 each); a delivery-grade 45 W bank plus trigger board and heavy-rated cables on the Pi 5 (−1).
12. **Session runtime** — stored energy versus draw: S3's small cell with duty-cycled draw lasts long (+1); P4's bank dwarfs its gentle draw (+1); both Pi branches net out ordinary — the Pi 5 from a very large bank against a heavy draw (0), the Pi 4 from a mid bank against a mid draw (0).
13. **Power-rail complexity** — how many negotiated-contract/delicate-cable mechanisms sit between battery and chip: S3's protected-cell chain (+1); P4's plain 5 V bank feed (+1); the Pi 4's plain 5 V/3 A contract — the branch's own words: the raspi-5 branch's most tangled block simply does not exist here (+1); the Pi 5's 5 V/5 A contract, which fails into throttling and needs a power-delivery trigger board and e-marked 5 A cables → −1.
14. **Recharge turnaround** — S3's small cell recharges quickly and cheaply to duplicate (+1); P4's swappable bank and the Pi 4's swappable bank (0 each); the Pi 5's multi-hour 20,000 mAh bank recharge (−1).
15. **Camera capture architecture** — how many native camera ports the board has: S3 has one (−1), P4 has one (−1), and the Pi 4 has one (−1) → all three leave the multiplex/alternation decision to the tracking gate at bench test 7, while the Pi 5's two native lanes close the question in hardware (+1). This is the row where the P4's and Pi 4's compute surplus is spent buying back a problem the S3 branch also carries — and on the Pi 4 it is the reopened risk the branch itself flags.
16. **Camera price and availability** — OV2640 modules cost ~₱100–200 each everywhere (+1); SC2336-class is available with fewer stockists and a lens-variant caution (0); the official Camera Module 3 Wide is 3–4× the price with shaky distributor stock → −1; the IMX219 160° modules sit at ₱500–900 each across **many confirmed local listings** — the availability term is strong enough to score +1 despite the mid price.
17. **Field-of-view fit** — 160° modules on S3 (+1) and 160° modules on the Pi 4 (+1 — the widest of the four); the P4's pinned sensor class lists narrow lenses by default, wide variants needing checkout care (0); the Pi 5's official 120° wide camera is a clean fit (+1).
18. **Camera software maturity** — the S3's camera driver and detection examples are the most trodden path in the Espressif ecosystem (+1); the P4's sensor support is in-tree but newer (0); on the Pi side the IMX219 is the longest-supported Raspberry Pi camera — mature drivers and a decade of examples (+1), and the Pi 5's official stack is likewise mature (+1).
19. **Audio path simplicity** — S3 and P4 both take the plan-native literal digital-audio-into-amplifier route into the mandatory wired jack (+1 each); the Pi 5's USB-dongle detour is **forced** (no analog output exists) → −1; the Pi 4's dongle path is a **consciously chosen** detour — the board's native jack exists and is declined for signal-to-noise and cross-branch consistency, so the fidelity term is fine and the complexity is a deliberate, documented trade → 0.
20. **Link implementation risk** — S3's ESP-NOW is native and abundant in examples (+1); the P4's answer — an on-board companion chip bridged by esp_hosted glue — is functional but is exactly the hop its own document flags for verification (−1); both Pi branches run the standard Linux access-point pattern, unremarkable (0 each).
21. **Link throughput headroom** — ESP-NOW suits today's small payloads but little more (0); the Wi-Fi-6 companion (+1) and both full Linux network stacks (+1, +1) leave room to grow.
22. **Total on-head weight** — the wearer carries everything: S3 well under ~200 g (+1); P4 and Pi 4 land in the ~300 g class with their banks (0 each — the Pi 4 trades the P4's big board for its heatsink); R5 goes past ~450 g with its cooler and biggest bank (−1).
23. **Breadboard-friendliness** — the Phase 2 rule forbids soldering: the S3 devkit is all header rows (+1); the P4 evaluation board is connector-saturated (serial-camera and flat-flex interfaces dominate) → −1; both Pi branches expose a 40-pin header workable with common cobbler adapters (0 each).
24. **Hand-solder carrier fit** — the Phase 3 build is outsourced to a local hand-solder service with carrier boards designed through-hole-dominant ([`assembly.md`](assembly.md) binds this): the S3 fits that bind perfectly (+1); on P4, R5, and the Pi 4 the compute board itself is a connector-saturated module that mounts as a unit, and the flat-flex/ribbon camera world sits at the edge of what a hand-solder shop wants — the harness work still applies to all four, but the carrier story is simplest on S3 (0, 0, 0).
25. **Strap-form fit** — the resolved form factor is an elastic strap with printed mounts, not a helmet: a credit-card-plus devkit vanishes into a mount (+1); the P4's evaluation board rides mid-strap, bulky but practical (0); both Pi boards are 85×56 mm slabs — the Pi 5 plus cooler plus the biggest bank is the least consistent with the form (−1), and the Pi 4 plus mandatory heatsink repeats the same structural mismatch with a lighter bank (−1).
26. **HRTF rendering capacity and quality-building headroom** — spatialized rendering is the product (`renderer`: generic HRTF azimuth, 48 kHz buffers under the ≤ 5 ms-per-buffer row), and the plan deliberately keeps the workload modest (elevation is carried by the carrier-pitch cue, not spectral HRTF — the plan's risk 3). All four run the planned HRTF; this variable scores what the chip can afford **beyond** that while sharing duty with vision: longer impulse responses and denser azimuth grids (the real levers on rendering quality), and eventually per-participant measured HRTF sets. S3: planned route fits inside the budget, but upgrade headroom while co-residing with detection is bounded, and measured-HRTF work is out of its class → 0. P4: vector instructions plus PSRAM tables afford longer filters and denser grids alongside CV duty (+1). Both Pi branches: CPU to burn for research-grade binaural work (+1 each). This is the one row where branch choice most directly touches the study's perceptual outcomes (the localization metrics) — a genuine plus for the three high-compute branches even though the thesis plan specifies the modest, robust route; worth putting before the co-researcher at sign-off rather than burying.
27. **Pointer-tracking (ArUco/AprilTag) detection-software readiness** — the vision tier's path from camera frames to quad detection to the pose solve that turns the printed marker's known physical size into bearing and range ([README §2.5](../README.md#25-pointer-tracking-stack-vision-uwb-imu), [§3.8](#38-pointer-tracking-hardware--notes--contingencies)). The row deliberately does **not** re-score raw compute margin (row 5), camera port count (row 15), or capture-driver maturity (row 18) — it isolates the one thing they do not say: whether the tracker is a library call or hand-built engineering. S3 and P4: no OpenCV exists on ESP-IDF, so the quad detector and the pose solve are hand-implemented in embedded C — the ecosystem's nearest stock precedent is a QR-code example (finder-pattern logic, not a fiducial pipeline); feasible, and the plan's own designed route (the marker decision explicitly moved the cost from hardware to compute), but custom engineering that must land inside the co-residence budget → 0 each; scoring S3 lower than P4 here would double-count row 5's margin. Both Pi branches: `cv2.aruco` (opencv-contrib) and mature AprilTag libraries make the plan's exact pipeline standard function calls on `picamera2` frames → +1 each. The row's honest message: **on the two Linux branches the vision tier is configuration; on the two microcontroller branches it is a build item** — where the tier's schedule risk lives before the pre-oral defense, mitigated by the plan's named fallbacks (flexible dictionary choice, the detect-then-track scheme, QVGA mono frames) rather than eliminated.

## 4. Reading the result

- **Unweighted: ESP32-S3 +19, ESP32-P4 +7, Raspberry Pi 4 +1, Raspberry Pi 5 −9.** Gate-weighted (rows 5, 6, 7, 15 doubled; row 27 single): **S3 +18, P4 +9, RPi 4 −1, RPi 5 −9** — the ordering survives both weightings, with one nuance: gate-weighting *lowers* the Pi 4 below zero (four of its weaknesses sit on gate-touching rows) while leaving the Pi 5 unchanged (its two native camera ports buy back one gate row), so the two Linux branches converge under stress — the Pi 4's price advantage does not convert into gate safety.
- **The tracking tier's software dimension is now explicit (row 27)**: on the two Linux branches the ArUco/AprilTag pointer tracker is a stock library call; on the two microcontroller branches it is a hand-built detection-and-pose pipeline (no OpenCV on ESP-IDF) that must be validated inside the co-residence gate — the strongest single argument for the high-compute branches once the HRTF row is on the table, with the plan's named fallbacks (flexible dictionary, detect-then-track, QVGA mono frames) mitigating rather than eliminating that schedule exposure.
- The corrected scoring semantics sharpen the story: the S3 leads not because the P4 is bad — its co-residence, heat, runtime, audio, power-simplicity, and HRTF-headroom scores are all genuinely +1 — but because the S3 wins or ties on nearly everything while being the cheapest at the head and in the pocket. The P4's honest deficit list is short and specific: one camera port, the companion-chip link hop, flat-cable-heavy bench ergonomics, thin supply lanes. The raspi-4 branch nets to exactly zero — it inherits the Linux class's structural costs (kernel-in-the-audio-path, heat with a mandatory heatsink, boot exposure, the single camera port, the slab form) while shedding the Pi 5's power-rail complexity and a third of its price, and it buys back camera availability with the IMX219 pair — the trade is real, but it nets to neither good nor poor. The Raspberry Pi 5's deficits — power, heat on the head, weight, boot/latency exposure, price — remain structural to the computing class, which is exactly what a head-mounted device cannot buy back with headroom.
- The matrix is a prior, not a verdict: the bench gates (render tests and the tracking co-residence test) are the referee on whatever board the co-researcher's sign-off picks. Prices were point-in-time (2026-09-26) and the ultra-wideband module's single-shop risk applies to all four equally, so it scores nowhere.
